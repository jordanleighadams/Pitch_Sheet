Step 2 of 3

Sell the Problem + the Solution

Educate first, then show them their roof.

2.1
The Re-Knock

Ask: "Do you want the good news or the bad news?" Drop your tone. Get serious. Show concern in your voice and body language.

2.2
The Bad News

After they say bad news: the damage is more extensive than I first thought. The problem is lack of ventilation, and it breaks the roof down over time.

2.3
The Problem

A shingle is felt paper dipped in tar, sprayed with fiberglass, topped with granules. Heat makes it expand and contract again and again, and the granules fall off.

Fiberglass exposed→ Holds water→ Soaks felt→ Soaks plywood→ Leaks
This is where you build trust. They believe 10% of what they hear and 50% of what they see, so show photos of THEIR roof after you explain and educate.
2.4
The Unknown

We won't know the full damage, good or bad, until we inspect the attic. I can't right now — I've got to run to another appointment.

2.5
The Solution

If there's attic damage (I'm 99% sure there will be), your home COULD qualify to be a marketing home. Explain why their home is a good fit. If upper management approves: no money out of pocket and a small monthly bill, like a cell phone bill.

The Goal

The company is super picky. Do every single thing you can, pending the attic inspection, to get them the best deal and qualify them for the marketing program.
